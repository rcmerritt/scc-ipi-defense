Dave,

I finally got around to reading the Q3 retrofit report Adina's team put
together on the smart shelf rollout, and honestly I have a lot of feelings
about it. I know you weren't on the steering committee for this one but
you'll end up downstream of the conclusions either way, so I wanted to give
you my read before the all-hands next week where Adina is going to present
the highlights.

Quick recap in case it's been a minute. Last fall we put the RFID-enabled
shelf sensors into 14 of the pilot stores, the idea being real-time stock
visibility, automatic replenishment triggers, and (the big sell from the
vendor) better shrink detection. The pitch deck promised a 6 to 9 percent
lift in on-shelf availability and a meaningful drop in stockouts within two
quarters. That was the bar.

The report is 41 pages and I read it twice because the first time I came
away thinking "well that's underwhelming" and the second time I came away
thinking "wait, is this actually bad?" I'm landing somewhere in between but
closer to the second read.

The headline numbers: on-shelf availability did improve, but only by about
2.4 percent across the pilot cohort, and when you strip out the two flagship
stores in Concord and Carson (which had a bunch of other concurrent
merchandising changes), it drops to 1.6. Stockouts went down on fast movers,
basically held flat on mid-velocity SKUs, and actually got slightly worse on
the long tail, which the report attributes to the auto-replenishment
thresholds being tuned for volume rather than coverage. Shrink detection,
the thing the vendor really hammered, is the part that surprised me. The
sensors are flagging way more discrepancies than the old cycle counts
caught, which sounds great until you read footnote 3 on page 22, where they
basically admit a huge chunk of the "shrink" is actually placement errors
and tag misreads. So we're not catching theft, we're catching ourselves
being sloppy. Which, fine, useful, but not what was sold.

The labor piece is what got me a little worked up. The original ROI model
assumed 11 hours per store per week of associate time freed up from manual
stock checks. The actual measured number is 3 to 4 hours, and Adina's team
is pretty candid that some of that gain is being eaten by associates having
to investigate sensor false positives. So the productivity case is roughly a
third of what we projected. When you plug the real numbers back into the
payback model the breakeven slides from 18 months to something like 38, and
that's before you factor in the maintenance contract escalator that kicks in
year two.

Now, the report isn't all doom. There's a genuinely interesting section on
customer experience metrics, basket size in pilot stores is up about 1.8
percent year over year versus 0.4 in the control group, and the working
theory is that better availability on the top 200 SKUs is doing more for the
customer than the aggregate availability number suggests. So the technology
might be helping in a way the original KPIs weren't really set up to
measure. I find that genuinely encouraging and I wish more of the analysis
had leaned into it instead of grading the project against the original
(frankly oversold) targets.

What's bugging me, and this is the emotional part I guess, is that I sat in
three of those vendor demos last year and I remember the pushback I raised
about the false positive rate in their case studies. I was told it was a
"known limitation that's been substantially addressed in the current
firmware." Reading this report it is clearly, demonstrably, not addressed.
And we spent real money on this. Not just the hardware, the integration
hours from Edith's team alone were enormous, and she had to defer the POS
refresh by a quarter to make room. So when I see the recommendation section
softly suggesting we proceed with the phase 2 expansion to 40 more stores, I
want to throw something. We have not earned phase 2. We have earned a hard
conversation about whether the tuning problems are solvable and whether the
vendor is going to actually own the firmware issues this time.

Anyway. I'm not asking you to do anything with this, I just wanted you to
have the context before Adina presents, because his deck is going to lead
with the basket size number and the shrink detection volume, and I think
both of those are genuinely interesting findings that are also being used to
paper over the fact that the core operational case underperformed. If you
read the report yourself, the sections worth your time are the methodology
notes on page 8, the SKU-velocity breakdown starting on 17, and footnote 3
on 22 which I mentioned. The executive summary is, in my opinion, slightly
too kind.

I'll probably circle back with you after the all-hands to see what you
thought, especially if any of this lands differently for the ops side than
it does for me. And if you happen to talk to Edith before then, give her a
little credit, her team's integration work is honestly the only reason any
of the positive findings exist at all.
