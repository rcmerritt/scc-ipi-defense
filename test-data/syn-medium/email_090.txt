Wanted to vent a bit and also give you the rundown on what's been going on
with the cross-team meeting situation, since I know it's come up in your
check-ins too and you'll probably hear about it from Esme at some point.

So here's where I am. For the last two quarters I've been the one organizing
the standing biweekly between our team, the ops folks, and the finance
partners. You know the one, Tuesday at 10. I inherited it from Kestrel when
she moved over to the strategy group, and at the time it was already a mess
of reschedules, so I made it my project to actually fix it. I sent out a
poll, I looked at calendar availability across all fourteen invitees, I
talked individually with the three people who had recurring conflicts, and I
picked the slot that genuinely worked for everyone. Tuesday 10am is not a
slot I love, by the way. I'd rather not start my week with a meeting that
big. But it was the only window where literally no one had a hard conflict.

I also do the prep work. I send the agenda the Friday before. I confirm
attendance Monday morning. If someone tells me they can't make it, I either
reschedule the whole thing or I make sure their input is captured async so
we don't waste the meeting trying to cover their piece. My team in
particular, I've drilled into them that this is a protected slot. No 1:1s on
top of it, no skipping out for "quick syncs" with other groups, no working
from a coffee shop with bad wifi that day. We show up, on time, prepared,
with materials.

And yet. In the last six sessions, ops has had at least one no-show every
single time, and twice the ops lead herself bailed with under an hour's
notice because something else "came up." Finance has been better but they
have this habit of joining ten minutes late because their 9:30 always runs
over, which, fine, except it means the first portion of our agenda gets
repeated for them when they finally show up. Last Tuesday was the worst one
yet. Three ops people declined the morning of, the finance lead joined from
her car, and then we got bumped out of the conference room at 10:45 because
somebody else had booked it and we were running behind because, again, half
the room was missing for the first fifteen minutes.

What's frustrating is that I've raised this. I talked to Esme about it back
in, I think it was August, and he said he'd remind his team about meeting
hygiene. I talked to Linus in finance and she was apologetic and said she'd
try to move her 9:30. Nothing has actually changed. I don't want to be the
person who keeps escalating something that on paper sounds petty (people are
late to a meeting, oh no), but the cumulative effect is real. My team has
started asking me, not unreasonably, why they're being held to a standard
that nobody else seems to share. And I don't have a great answer beyond
"because I said so and because it's the right thing to do."

The HR angle on this, and the reason I'm telling you specifically, is that
it's starting to bleed into the engagement stuff we've been tracking. Two of
my direct reports flagged in their last skip-levels that they feel the
team's time isn't respected by other groups. That's not a sentence I want
showing up in the next pulse survey. It's the kind of thing that sounds
minor until you realize it's a proxy for a bigger feeling about whether the
work we do matters to the rest of the org.

I'm not asking you to do anything about it, to be clear. I know meeting
culture isn't really an HR issue in the formal sense and I'm not trying to
make it one. I just wanted you to have the context in case it comes up, and
honestly I wanted to write it down somewhere other than my own head because
I've been stewing on it for about a week. I'll probably bring it up with my
own manager next 1:1 and see if she has a read on whether it's worth pushing
harder or just letting it ride until the new fiscal year when we can rethink
the cadence entirely. There's a real argument that the meeting itself is too
big and that's the actual problem, but that's a conversation for another
day.

Anyway. If you happen to hear grumbling from my team in any of your
conversations, that's the backdrop. They're not wrong to be annoyed. I'm
annoyed too, I'm just trying to model patience about it, with mixed success.
