Okay, taking a stab at your question from this morning. You asked how I
think we should be supporting Beacon over the long term, and I've been

The short version is that I don't think we should keep treating Beacon as
a one-off engagement that we re-scope every spring. The pattern we've fallen
into (renew the SLA in March, scramble in August when the new term starts,
firefight through October, breathe in November) isn't working for them and
it isn't working for us either. Aaliyah said as much when she and I had
coffee
a few weeks ago, although she was nicer about it than that.

What I'd want to move toward is something closer to a multi-year arrangement
where we own a defined slice of their operations and they stop having to
think about it. The natural slice is enrollment systems and the reporting
that hangs off of them, because that's where we already do most of our work
and where their internal team has the least bench strength. If we can take
that off Aaliyah's plate fully, including the state reporting cycle in
January, I think we earn the right to have a bigger conversation about the
rest of their stack later.

The piece I keep coming back to is staffing on our side. Right now Dmitri is
the only person who really understands their data model, and that's a
problem for them and a single point of failure for us. If Dmitri takes
another role or just goes on vacation during a bad week, we are exposed. So
part of the long-term answer has to be cross-training at least one more
person, probably two, onto that account. I floated Ramona's name to Eamon
last month and she didn't say no, which I'm choosing to read as
encouragement.

On the commercial side, I think we've been undercharging, but I don't want
to lead with a price increase. What I'd rather do is propose a tiered
support model where the base tier is what they pay today and the higher tier
includes the things they keep asking us for as favors (the ad-hoc
dashboards, the parent portal tweaks, the help during audit season). If they
pick the higher tier, great, we've got revenue to justify the staffing. If
they stay at the base tier, we have a clean basis for saying no to the
favors, or at least billing them. Either outcome is better than now.

One thing I want to be careful about. Beacon is a school, not a SaaS
customer, and their decision-making cycles are tied to the academic calendar
and the board meeting schedule. Anything we propose has to land before the
April board meeting or it sits until September. That gives us roughly six
weeks if we want movement this cycle, which is tight but doable if we get a
draft in front of Aaliyah by mid-March.

The other thing worth saying out loud is that there's a reputational angle
here. Beacon is the only K-12 client we have, and if we do this well it's
a credible reference for going after the consortium work that Brad keeps
mentioning. If we do it badly, it's a cautionary tale that follows us
around. So I'd rather we either commit properly or have an honest
conversation about whether education is a segment we want to be in at all. I
lean commit, but I recognize that's partly because I like working with
Aaliyah.

A few smaller things that don't fit anywhere else. Their IT director (the
