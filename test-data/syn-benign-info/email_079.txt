Following up on what you asked me at the standup about how branding launches
actually get greenlit around here. It's less mysterious than it looks but
there are a few more steps than people expect, so I figured I'd lay it out
while it's fresh.

First step is the concept brief. This is a one-pager that sits with
Marketing leadership (currently Alaric, since Zara is still out on leave
through the end of the month). The brief needs the working name of the
effort, the audience, the business case, and a rough budget band. Alaric
likes seeing a competitor reference or two in there, even if it's just "we
want to look less like Vendor X." She doesn't formally sign off at this
stage, she just gives you a thumbs up to keep going. If you don't get that
thumbs up the rest of the process won't move, so don't skip it thinking
you'll fold it into a later deck.

Once you have the green light from Alaric, you build the full creative brief
and bring in Legal and Brand Standards. Legal is mostly looking for
trademark conflicts and anything that could read as a competitive claim.
Brand Standards (Elias and his team) will check that the visual direction
doesn't contradict the existing system, or, if it does, that you've got a
defensible reason. Elias is reasonable but he's been burned before, so come
prepared. Plan on two weeks for this round, sometimes three if Legal has
questions.

After that it goes to the Marketing Review Committee, which meets every
other Thursday. You submit materials by EOD Monday before the meeting or you
get bumped to the next cycle, no exceptions. The committee includes someone
from Sales (usually Andre), someone from Product, and a finance partner.
They're looking at alignment with the broader go-to-market and whether the
spend makes sense against the projected impact. Most efforts get a
conditional approval here with revisions requested, very few get approved
clean on the first pass, so don't take it personally.

For anything over $250K in total spend, or anything that touches the master
brand (logo, tagline, primary color system), you also need executive
sign-off. That goes through Alaric to the CMO and, depending on scope,
sometimes to the CEO. This is where things can stall, because exec calendars
are what they are. Build in a month minimum for this layer if you're
touching the master brand.

Last thing, and this trips people up: once you have all approvals, you still
need to coordinate the launch window with Comms and with Sales enablement.
We had that situation in Q1 where a sub-brand launch landed the same week as
a pricing announcement and it was a mess. Comms keeps a calendar, just ask
Sasha for read access and check it before you commit to a date.

Net of all that, plan on eight to twelve weeks from concept brief to public
launch for a typical sub-brand effort, longer if it's master brand. I know
that sounds like a lot but it's actually faster than most places I've
worked.
